OCR GUIDE

How to Extract Text from an Image with OCR

OCR, or optical character recognition, turns readable text inside an image or scan into editable text. It can be useful for screenshots, photographed documents, receipts, forms and scanned pages.

Before you start

Use the clearest image you have. Text should be large enough to read, reasonably sharp, and not heavily covered by shadows, reflections or other objects. A straight, well-lit document usually produces better OCR than a tilted or blurry photo.

How to extract text

  1. Open the free OCR tool.
  2. Choose a clear JPG, PNG or supported image.
  3. Run the OCR process and wait for the extracted text.
  4. Read through the result and correct names, numbers, punctuation and unusual characters.
  5. Copy or download the text for your next task.

How to improve OCR accuracy

  • Crop away unrelated areas around the document.
  • Use a higher-resolution image when small characters are difficult to read.
  • Avoid extreme compression that makes letters blocky or blurry.
  • Keep the page as straight as possible.
  • Always verify important numbers, names and addresses against the original image.

OCR vs Image to Text

OCR and image-to-text usually describe the same general workflow: reading characters from pixels and returning editable text. FreeTools AI provides both an OCR tool and an Image to Text tool so you can choose the workflow that fits your task.

Frequently asked questions

Can OCR read a photo of a document?

Yes, if the text is sufficiently clear. Sharpness, lighting, resolution and contrast all affect the result.

Why does OCR make mistakes?

OCR can struggle with blurry images, unusual fonts, low contrast, handwriting, perspective distortion and characters that look similar. Review the extracted text before using it.

Is OCR useful for scanned PDFs?

Yes. If a PDF contains scanned pages rather than selectable text, converting the pages to images and using OCR can make the text easier to reuse.
